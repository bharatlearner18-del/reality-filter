Step 1 of 4

Every week another AI tool launches claiming to change everything. Reality Filter tells you whether it actually does — one clear answer, backed by evidence we name and sources we quote, so you can decide in about a minute instead of an afternoon.

The problem it solves

Every AI launch sounds identical. “10× faster.” “Beats every benchmark.” “This changes everything.” Some of it is true. A lot of it isn't. And the only way to find out has been to spend twenty or thirty minutes yourself, digging through benchmark tables, developer threads and reviews, before you can decide whether to switch to it, pay for it, or ignore it.

Reality Filter does that digging and hands you the conclusion.

What you actually do

You type the name of an AI tool, model or app into the search box — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Cursor, or whatever you saw people posting about this morning. That's the entire input. There's no sign-up, no account, and nothing to install.

What you get back

One answer, out of four. Not a score, not a wall of research — a decision:

  • WORTH TRYING NOW

    It does what it says. Go ahead and use it.

  • WATCH LATER

    It's real, but there are catches worth knowing before you commit.

  • TOO EARLY

    It might turn out fine. The evidence just isn't in yet.

  • MOSTLY FOG MACHINE

    The claims run well ahead of anything anyone can show.

Alongside it you get a hype risk reading — a plain sentence on how much of the story is still unproven — and a short note on what you should do next, written separately for people building with it and people buying it.

Where the answer comes from

Every verdict is researched by hand from current sources, and it names them. Actual benchmark names and scores. Named reviewers and independent testers. What real users and companies report after living with the thing. You'll never read “reports suggest” — if we can't point at who said it, it doesn't go in.

We also publish the evidence against our own call when it exists. A verdict you can only agree with isn't worth much.

The full evidence sits behind one click, deliberately. The answer comes first so you can decide in about a minute — the proof is there underneath for when you want to check our work rather than take our word for it.

Why you can trust it

We keep a public, dated track record — a small, hand-picked set of calls, a few added each month, each one published before anyone knew who'd turn out to be right. Not every verdict goes on it, deliberately: it's a record of the calls we choose to stand behind, not a log of everything we've ever looked up. The ones that do go on it stay there, and we don't quietly edit them — when the facts change we re-check the tool, update the verdict, and leave the original call standing next to the correction.

That has already happened: GPT-5.6 was filed as “too early”, then moved to “worth trying” when it became generally available — and the original call is still quoted inside the packet rather than deleted. We've also called a product doing $1.2B a year “mostly fog machine”. If this only ever agreed with press releases, it would be worthless to you.

What it costs

Nothing. Reading any verdict is free and needs no account. If we haven't checked something yet, you can leave an email and a real person researches it by hand and sends you the answer, usually within a day.
